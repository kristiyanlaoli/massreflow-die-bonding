# Educational model

- Bump and pitch inputs are physically expressed in µm. The 3D view uses a sampled 5×5 array; diameter/pitch ratio changes the displayed bumps, but the number of bumps and visible spacing are not a literal full die layout. Offset is magnified consistently with the pitch. A 3×3 die coupon and adaptive tape/ring make pickup visible. Ejector lift, die rotation and placement head handoff are synchronized by a shared normalized timeline; the tool geometry is schematic.
- Reference solder: illustrative SAC-type solder with liquidus about 217 °C. The profile allocates a normalized above-liquidus interval from the requested TAL; peaks at/below liquidus have no above-liquidus segment. Actual reflow time and temperature are not validated. The model uses a heuristic collapse factor from peak temperature and TAL and a cold-reflow/open-risk indicator; it is not a melting/solidification solver. The conveyor depicts three identical representative packages heated together.
- Joint stand-off, bridging/open indicators, residue, underfill coverage/voids, damage and delamination risk are illustrative functions of the parameter values. The percentages are heuristic indicators, not observed defect rates or probabilities. Defect geometry is representative (not a prediction of location/count). Underfill is dispensed from an edge after cleaning; a moving fill front illustrates capillary flow, not CFD.
- PASS/FAIL criteria are internal educational assumptions. Pre-cure results project the completed recipe; during cure they reflect simulated cure progress. No industrial qualification standard is claimed. Underfill relative amount and flux relative amount use 100% as a nominal reference, not physical volume. 

- v1.2 area flux spray: a single broad nozzle/manifold emits fine mist across the full bonding region. A continuous thin translucent film represents nominal flux application before chip placement, persists through pickup/alignment/placement, and fades as solder joins during reflow. Flux coating opacity/thickness varies with relative amount (20–160%). This is a schematic visual approximation; it does not solve droplet aerodynamics, film thickness, chemistry, oxidation, or wetting. A flux-compatible spray process and amount must be qualified; not every flip-chip flow uses spraying.
